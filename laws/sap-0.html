SAP-0 — Substrate Alignment Principle
A formal RD-0 law defining when linguistic inference systems achieve maximal reasoning fidelity through structural alignment between a domain and its linguistic representation substrate.
STATUS: ACTIVE CORE LAW
LAW STATEMENT

Formal Definition

An LLM achieves maximal reasoning fidelity when the representational structure of a domain is isomorphic to its native linguistic token space.

In simplified terms, reasoning performance increases as structural alignment between domain representation and language representation increases.

INFERENCE MODEL

RD-0 Structural Mapping

The inference process operates across three primary transformation spaces:

E (External Domain Space) ↓ encode L (Linguistic Token Space) ↓ process I (Internal Model Space) ↓ decode E' (Reconstructed / Predicted Domain)

At each transformation boundary, representational distortion may occur, reducing downstream inference fidelity.

LAW CONDITION

Alignment Function

Define A(E, L) as the structural alignment function between domain and language representation.

SAP-0: Performance ∝ A(E, L)

Where A increases as translation loss decreases and structural isomorphism increases.

HIGH ALIGNMENT DOMAIN

Maximal Fidelity Conditions

Domains that are already native to linguistic structure (mathematics, code, formal logic, structured legal systems) exhibit high inference stability.

In these cases, E ≈ L and transformation overhead approaches minimal loss.

LOW ALIGNMENT DOMAIN

Degraded Fidelity Conditions

Physical, sensory, or high-dimensional systems require compression into language, introducing representational distortion and probabilistic drift.

These systems exhibit increased uncertainty under token-based inference.

COROLLARIES

Derived System Rules

COR-1: Compression efficiency directly increases reasoning stability.

COR-2: Translation loss is proportional to inference degradation.

COR-3: Fully linguistic domains form closed inference loops with minimal entropy leakage.
