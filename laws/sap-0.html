RD-0 CORE LAW • SAP-0
Substrate Alignment Principle
A formal RD-0 law defining when linguistic inference systems achieve maximal reasoning fidelity through structural alignment between domain representation and token substrate.
LAW STATEMENT

Formal Definition

An LLM achieves maximal reasoning fidelity when the representational structure of a domain is isomorphic to its native linguistic token space.

In simplified form: reasoning performance increases as structural alignment between domain and language increases.

RD-0 MODEL

System Mapping

The inference system operates across three spaces:

E (External Domain Space) ↓ encode L (Linguistic Token Space) ↓ process I (Internal Model Space) ↓ decode E' (Reconstructed Domain)

Where degradation occurs at each non-isomorphic transformation boundary.

LAW CONDITION

Alignment Function

Let A(E, L) represent structural alignment between domain and language.

SAP-0: Performance ∝ A(E, L)

Where A increases as translation loss decreases and structural similarity increases.

INTERPRETATION

High Alignment Domains

Domains such as mathematics, code, logic, and structured language systems exhibit high fidelity because E ≈ L.

Low Alignment Domains

Physical, sensory, and high-dimensional real-world systems require compression into language, introducing structural distortion and inference drift.

COROLLARIES

Derived Rules

COR-1: Compression efficiency increases reasoning stability.

COR-2: Translation loss reduces inference accuracy proportionally.

COR-3: Language-contained systems form closed reasoning loops.
